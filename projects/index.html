Projects

My research asks how robots can adapt motion priors to changing commands, terrain, and physical conditions. These studies explore three approaches: optimizing the parameters of a CPG-based controller, adding residual corrections to learned animal skills, and generating terrain-aware references from human motion. The common aim is to preserve useful movement patterns while allowing the robot to respond to its surroundings.

2024 · EPFL BioRob

Online adaptation of CPG parameters

Controllers based on central pattern generators (CPGs) encode rhythmic locomotion with a small set of parameters. This work uses Bayesian optimization to update those parameters online, balancing velocity tracking and energy use.

Contextual information helps the optimizer respond to changes in slope and payload, while reusing previous trials supports changes in target velocity. Experiments in simulation and on a Unitree Go1 evaluate adaptation to velocity commands, ground friction, slope, and payload. The controller retains its oscillator structure while learning parameter settings through interaction.

2025 · ETH Zurich RSL

Residual adaptation of animal motion priors

Dog motion-capture data provide demonstrations of locomotion on flat ground. A low-level policy learns to imitate these motions and is then frozen, preserving a reusable set of movement skills.

A goal-conditioned high-level policy supplies latent motion commands and adds joint-level residual corrections for uneven terrain and obstacle avoidance. Training penalizes large residuals to balance adaptation with fidelity to the motion prior. Hardware experiments on ANYmal-D demonstrate stair traversal and local navigation among obstacles.

2026 · ETH Zurich RSL

Terrain-aware generation from human motion priors

Human motion provides coordinated whole-body demonstrations, but fixed references limit adaptation to new terrain. This framework trains a diffusion model on retargeted human motion to generate references conditioned on terrain, target direction, and recent robot states.

An RL-trained tracker executes these references and is fine-tuned in closed loop with the generator frozen. Terrain observations also allow the tracker to adjust execution when references are imperfect. The system does not add a separate residual adaptation policy. Hardware experiments on the Unitree G1 demonstrate box climbing, hurdle traversal, stairs, and mixed-terrain transitions, with perception and computation onboard.
