Motion Priors Reimagined: Adapting Flat-Terrain Skills for Complex Quadruped Mobility

Zewei Zhang, Chenhao Li, Takahiro Miki, Marco Hutter

Flat-ground dog motion becomes a reusable skill layer, with learned joint corrections for rough terrain and local navigation.

Reading the results

The quoted 75–95 successes in 100 trials evaluate the student policy with noisy observations in simulation. Hardware demonstrations show deployment on ANYmal-D. The paper also reports that the high-level policy can favor one gait rather than reliably switch between all learned gaits.

The key ideas

01

Learn a movement prior

A low-level policy learns to imitate dog motion on flat ground. It is then frozen, preserving a reusable set of movement skills.

02

Adapt through residuals

A goal-conditioned high-level policy supplies latent motion commands and joint-level residual corrections. Penalizing large residuals balances terrain adaptation with fidelity to the learned movement patterns.

03

Transfer to the robot

A student learns from the teacher using noisy simulated observations, before deployment on the real robot. Hardware experiments demonstrate stair traversal and local navigation among obstacles.
